Find the pattern in the stars.

Turn a color FITS image into an instrumental color–magnitude diagram.

Also available: the StackImages HR diagram

HR Diagram of Your Own Image runs the HR diagram from Robert Vanderbei’s StackImages desktop app in the browser, with the same results as the desktop app. Both pages read your FITS image on your own computer. They measure stars differently, so the same image gives different diagrams:

For dense globular clusters such as M2 or M13, the StackImages version usually gives a cleaner diagram. Its first diagram of a large image can take a minute.

Choose your image

Use a linear, three-channel RGB FITS image saved from Siril. A display autostretch is fine; the saved pixel values should remain linear.

Your image is processed on this computer.

Image size and browser memory

Limits: 19 megapixels (width × height) and 250 MiB per RGB FITS file. The tested M13 classroom example is 6.05 megapixels and about 69 MiB.

Processing can use about 1.5 GiB for an image near the limits, so an image within the limits may still exceed your browser’s capacity, especially on phones and tablets. If processing fails, close unused tabs or try the course example or a smaller crop.

Colors and magnitudes are instrumental measurements. The brightest measured star
        is assigned magnitude 10 before color filtering. This scale measures relative brightness;
        it does not provide absolute magnitude or stellar temperature.

      

        How the measurements are selected
        
The tool uses the supplied course script’s detection and aperture settings. It excludes
          edge detections and stars with nonpositive RGB flux, then removes the lowest and highest
          1% of measured colors. Saturation and overlapping stars can affect the measurements.

        
CSV positions use zero-based pixel coordinates. The plot and CSV contain the same stars.
